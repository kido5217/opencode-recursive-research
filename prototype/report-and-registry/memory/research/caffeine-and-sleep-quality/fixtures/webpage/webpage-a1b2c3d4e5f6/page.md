# Caffeine effects on sleep taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before going to bed

*Illustrative sample fixture — text abbreviated for the prototype, not a real capture.*

**Background.** Caffeine is the most widely consumed psychoactive substance, and its
disruption of sleep is well documented at short intervals before bedtime.

**Methods.** Double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study in healthy adults.
Participants received 400 mg caffeine at 0, 3, or 6 hours before habitual bedtime.

**Results.** Compared with placebo, caffeine significantly reduced total sleep time
at all three intervals. The 0-hour condition showed the largest reduction; even the
6-hour condition reduced sleep by more than one hour on average.

**Conclusions.** Caffeine consumed up to 6 hours before bedtime can meaningfully
disrupt sleep, and the effect is measurable even when the drug is no longer
subjectively felt.
